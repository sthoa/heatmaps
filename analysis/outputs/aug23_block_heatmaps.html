Aug 23 Centre-Injection Heatmaps

6 h run, 0.4% agarose, non-BSA, nanoparticles injected into the centre gap. Comparisons: PEG vs COOH coating, and large vs small magnet against no-magnet controls.

How to read: one panel per coating; rows are the large-magnet arm (mean of L1–L3), the small-magnet arm (mean of S1–S3), and the no-magnet control; columns step through the run. Each cell is the 10×10 mm block seen from above — the x-axis spans the whole block with 0 mm at the left edge (where a back-injection gap would sit) and the magnet side at the right, so the centre injection gap appears as the bright band near 5 mm. Colour is NP darkness above each frame’s own gel floor, so camera exposure cancels; one shared scale (0–22 L*) across both panels. The outer ~0.8 mm of each block is trimmed because the gel pulls away from the holder late in the run and that seam shadow would read as nanoparticles.

Caveat: repeats 1–2 were photographed on 23 Aug and repeat 3 plus both controls on 25–26 Aug, so each magnet row averages across two sessions (see the note below).
Geometry update (31 Aug). Block corners now come from the holder’s corner marks with series-median outlier repair: the block does not change size between shots, so a quad disagreeing with its series’ median size or shape is a detection failure and is replaced by the median shape at that frame’s own centroid. Measured on JPEG re-encoded copies of every photo, this halves the geometry noise floor (0.0026 → 0.0015 mm median centroid reproducibility) and removes a smear artefact previously visible in the PEG small-magnet 4 h panel. Per-frame ECC registration (the Aug 27 approach) was tested and rejected — it made reproducibility worse, not better. Conclusions are unchanged.

What the panels show

Transport is unambiguous and ordered large magnet > small magnet > control for both coatings. Quantified as the NP signal on the magnet side (6–9.2 mm) minus the far side (0.8–4 mm), in L* units:

Both arms rise monotonically all run and never cross; controls sit at zero throughout — without a magnet the band stays put, which is the cleanest possible confirmation that the asymmetry in the magnet arms is magnetic transport rather than diffusion or drift. The large magnet drives faster and further transport than the small one at every timepoint; the PEG-vs-COOH difference is within repeat scatter at n = 3.
