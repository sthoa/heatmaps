STAGE 1

What the raw data actually is

In: phone photos  ·  Out: a table of 1005 rows

Every measurement in this project comes from top-view photographs. Nothing was measured with an instrument; the camera is the instrument. That is the single most important thing to understand about the analysis, and most of the pipeline exists to make a phone camera behave like a repeatable one.

A “series” is one physical sample followed through time — for example COOH_non-BSA_r2. Each series contributes one point to any statistical test, never one point per photo. Photos of the same sample at different times are repeated measurements of one thing, not independent samples, and treating them as independent would inflate every significance figure enormously.

STAGE 2

Turning file names into timepoints

In: PXL_20260823_143012.jpg  ·  Out: hours since the magnet went on

Phone filenames carry a capture timestamp. The catalogue scripts parse it, take the earliest magnet-attached photo of each run as t = 0, and express every other photo as elapsed hours. Photos were then snapped to the nominal timepoint they belong to (0, 0.5, 1.0 h …), because you photographed each condition in sequence and the real capture times within one round differ by a few minutes.

Timepoints are therefore accurate to roughly the time it took to photograph one full round, not to the second. Over a 6-hour experiment with 30-minute sampling this is immaterial; it would matter if we were quoting a rate over minutes.

STAGE 3

Reading which sample each photo shows

In: photo  ·  Out: coating, BSA state, repeat number, magnet present

The condition was never recorded in a spreadsheet — it exists only as writing in the photograph. So the labels were read from the images themselves: coating and BSA state from the marker text on the sticker, repeat number from the tally marks on the magnet strap, magnet presence from the photo itself.

Two features of the rig made this hard, and both were dealt with explicitly:

  • Red marker on an orange sticker has almost no contrast. Before reading, each sticker crop was transformed to a* − 0.8 b* in LAB space, percentile-stretched, inverted and CLAHE-equalised. That turns marginal text crisp.
  • The ribbed magnet strap splits one tally stroke into two or three thin lines, so naive stroke counting over-reads. Counts were made on stroke groups, then refereed.

How label errors were caught

The design provides a constraint that the reading process does not know about: at every timepoint, the three repeats of a condition must be exactly {1, 2, 3}. Any trio that came back as {1, 2, 2} or {2, 3, 3} is provably wrong, and was re-read against reference crops of each repeat's own tally mark. On Aug 26, 23 of 52 trios needed this referee pass and 25 individual readings were corrected — nearly all “II read as III”. Every trio on every day now resolves cleanly.

A real error this caught

At the 1 h timepoint on Aug 26, the PEG BSA trio had been read one-too-high, which made it look as though repeat 1 was missing. You identified that repeat 3 was the actual gap and supplied the photo. After that, every classification was re-keyed by file path rather than by row index — adding one photo shifts every subsequent index and would silently reassign conditions to the wrong images.

STAGE 4

Finding the agarose block in the photo

In: photo, downscaled to 1100 px on the long edge  ·  Out: four corner coordinates

To compare a spot in one photo with the same spot in another, the block has to be located in both. Each day needed a different method, because each day's rig offers different reliable features. This is the part of the pipeline that changed most, and the current state is:

DayHow the block is foundWhy not something simpler
Aug 23The corner marks you drew on the holder, plus series-median outlier repair Colour segmentation is unstable here; and a quad that disagrees with its own series' median size is a detection failure, since the block cannot change size between shots
Aug 26The four red corner dots on the holder The magnet's warm reflections and the orange sticker both overlap the gel's colour range, so colour alone fails
Aug 27Geometry detected once per series from its t=0 frame, then every later frame registered onto it by ECC Back injection gives a rigid white rim to anchor on; registering absorbs per-frame detection noise instead of propagating it

The four corners are then mapped onto a square by a perspective transform — not a rotation or a bounding box. This matters: when the phone is tilted, the square block photographs as a trapezoid, and correcting it with a rotated rectangle shears the image so that the injection gap comes out leaning. You caught exactly that on the COOH L3 validation frames, which is why the true four-corner transform is used everywhere now.

STAGE 5

Turning pixels into a nanoparticle signal

In: warped 480 × 480 image  ·  Out: a grid of “excess darkness” values

Magnetite nanoparticles are dark brown; agarose is pale. Where particles accumulate, the gel gets darker. The measurement is that darkening, in four steps:

  1. Convert to LAB and keep L*, the lightness channel. LAB separates lightness from colour, so the measure responds to how dark a region is rather than to its hue. Darkness is defined as 255 − L*.
  2. Median-bin in 11 × 11 pixel blocks. The median, not the mean, so that a speck of dust, a bubble or a specular highlight cannot pull a cell.
  3. Subtract this frame's own gel floor — the 15th percentile of the block's interior. This is the step that makes photos comparable at all (see below).
  4. Light Gaussian smoothing (σ = 1 bin) so the heatmaps read as fields rather than as mosaics.

Why the floor subtraction is the important step

A phone camera sets its own exposure for every shot. Across this dataset the absolute lightness of an identical gel varies by roughly ±30 L* purely from auto-exposure. Any measurement of raw darkness would be dominated by that, not by nanoparticles.

Subtracting each frame's own 15th-percentile interior value removes it. The quantity being reported is therefore always darkness above the clean gel in the same photograph — never darkness compared to some other photograph. It is a within-frame measurement, which is what makes a phone camera usable as an instrument here.

The 15th percentile is used rather than the minimum because the minimum is a single pixel and therefore noise; the 15th percentile is the darkness of clean gel in a frame where at most most of the field is affected. It is a robust estimate of “background”.

STAGE 6

The heatmaps

In: excess-darkness grid per photo  ·  Out: the panels in your report

Each heatmap cell is the mean of the three repeats of that condition at that timepoint. The x-axis is millimetres across the block, with x = 0 at the block's left edge — where a back-injection gap would sit — so that centre-injection days put the gap near 5 mm and all three days share one spatial convention.

All three days use one colour scale, 0 to 55 L*. This is deliberate and worth being able to defend: an earlier version auto-scaled each panel to its own range, which made the Aug 26 panels look dimmer than Aug 23 even though Aug 26 has the strongest signal of the three days. You noticed. Auto-scaling makes panels prettier and makes them lie about relative magnitude, so it was removed.

The one heatmap artefact to declare

BSA gel is intrinsically cloudier than non-BSA gel — raw darkness around 127 against 67. On the shared scale, BSA controls therefore look nearly as bright as BSA magnet arms. That is turbidity, not transport. For BSA conditions the heatmap is qualitative and the asymmetry metric below is the quantity to trust, because cloudiness affects both halves of the block equally and cancels in a difference.

STAGE 7

The asymmetry metric

In: excess-darkness grid  ·  Out: one number per photo, in L*

This is the number the line graph plots and the statistics test. Definition:

gap position
the brightest column band in the middle 40% of the block — the reservoir is the brightest feature there
exclusion
±1.0 mm around the gap belongs to neither side, so the reservoir itself cannot drive the result
asymmetry
mean excess darkness of everything to the magnet side, minus mean excess darkness of everything to the far side

The reason for a difference rather than a raw amount is that it cancels everything symmetric. Gel cloudiness, residual exposure differences, BSA turbidity, uneven lighting across the whole block — all of these darken both halves and subtract out. What cannot cancel is material that has actually moved to one side. The magnet is on one side, so directional movement is the signal and everything else is common-mode rejection.

This also gives the metric a built-in null test. A no-magnet control has no reason to be asymmetric, so its curve should sit at zero — and that is a check that runs on every single plot, rather than a calibration done once.

What the controls actually did

All four non-BSA controls across both days stayed within ±4.4 L* of zero for the full six hours, with no trend. The magnet arms reach 13 to 42 L*. The separation between signal and null is roughly a factor of five, which is why the existence of transport is not in question even though the comparisons between conditions are.

STAGE 8

From a curve to one number per sample

In: 13 timepoints per series  ·  Out: one plateau value per series

Every curve rises and then flattens by about three hours. The summary value for a series is the mean of its 5 h and 6 h points — its plateau height.

Averaging two timepoints rather than taking the single 6 h value halves the effect of one noisy final photograph. The plateau was chosen rather than a rate because the curves are visibly flat by then, so the value is stable; an early-slope measure was also computed and gives the same ordering with larger scatter.

This collapses 13 photographs into one number precisely so that the statistics treat the sample as the unit of replication, not the photograph.

STAGE 9

The statistics behind p = 0.045

Because there are two factors crossed with each other, this is a 2 × 2 factorial and is tested as one model rather than as separate comparisons. A two-way ANOVA asks three questions of these twelve numbers at once: does concentration matter, does coating matter, and does the effect of one depend on the other.

Why this differs from what I told you earlier

Earlier in this analysis the same data was tested with separate pairwise t-tests, one contrast at a time, and nothing reached significance. That was a defect of the test, not a property of the data. Each pairwise test estimated noise from only the two or three samples in the pair, leaving two to four degrees of freedom; the factorial model estimates noise from all twelve samples at once and has eight. Same numbers, more of them used to measure the noise, so a real effect of this size becomes detectable.

What p = 0.045 does and does not mean

It means: if agarose concentration made no difference whatsoever, data this lopsided would turn up about 4.5% of the time by chance. It is weak-to-moderate evidence, sitting just inside the conventional threshold. It is not a strong result and should not be described as one.

It also says nothing about why. In this comparison 0.4% is Aug 23 and 0.6% is Aug 26, so “concentration” and “run day” are the same variable. The statistics establish that the two groups differ; they cannot establish that the gel is the reason. That limitation is not fixable by any analysis — only by running both concentrations on one day.

The 0.6% coating comparison

Within Aug 26 alone — one day, no cross-day confound — COOH leads PEG by +12.0 L*, F = 4.44, p = 0.064, 95% confidence interval [−0.9, +24.9], with BSA state included as a blocking factor. The BSA block is itself inert (p = 0.69), which is what justifies pooling the BSA and non-BSA arms into one test of coating.

The confidence interval crosses zero, which is the same statement as p > 0.05. The honest summary is a consistent direction with an effect size that could plausibly be anywhere from nothing to very large.

CHECKS

How we know the pipeline isn't inventing this

Four independent checks, none of which depend on the result coming out a particular way.

1 · Re-encode reproducibility

Every photo was re-saved as a JPEG — a perturbation of about 1.2 grey levels out of 255, invisible to the eye — and the entire analysis re-run. A pipeline that is measuring the sample rather than the compression should return the same answer. Median change in the derived depth measure: 0.0015 mm, against effects of 0.1 to 0.16 mm per hour. The measurement is roughly a hundred times more stable than the effects being reported.

2 · Two independent geometries agree

The Aug 23 block was located two completely different ways — by gel colour, and by the corner marks on the holder. Per-sample migration rates from the two methods correlate at r = 0.971, and the ordering of conditions is identical under both. The result does not depend on how the block was found.

3 · Controls sit at zero

Described above: ±4.4 L* with no trend, against signals of 13 to 42 L*. Nothing in the pipeline forces this — controls go through exactly the same code.

4 · A metric reproduces a previously published value

The back-injection depth measure, written independently for this comparison, recovers the Aug 27 magnet effect at +0.41 mm against the previously reported +0.44 mm.

LIMITS

Everything wrong with this analysis

Stated plainly, so none of it can be produced as a surprise.

  • Agarose concentration is confounded with run day. 0.4% is Aug 23 and 0.6% is Aug 26. Different gel batch, different session lighting. This is the most serious limitation and no analysis fixes it.
  • n = 3. Three samples per cell. One 0.6% COOH repeat plateaus at 12.8 L* and another at 48.1 — that spread is the reason most contrasts miss significance.
  • The coating × concentration interaction is not supported (p = 0.38). The coating gap does look wider at 0.6% (+17.9 vs +6.4 L*), but that specific comparison is the one that fails.
  • One cell reverses the coating ordering. Aug 23 small magnet: COOH 7.8, PEG 11.8. It is the cell with the least transport of any, but it exists.
  • The back-injection run cannot test any of this. Its own magnet effect is only +0.41 mm at p = 0.26, so there is nothing there to modulate.
  • Darkness is a proxy, not a concentration. No calibration curve relates L* to mg/mL of nanoparticles, and the relationship is certainly not linear at high loading, where the gel saturates towards black. All claims are about more or less, never how much.
  • This is a top view of a 3D block. The measurement integrates through the depth of the gel, so it cannot distinguish particles spread through the thickness from particles concentrated at the surface or on the bottom.
  • Timepoints are nominal, accurate to the few minutes it took to photograph a round.

If someone challenges you

The questions most likely to come, and the answers the data actually supports.

How do you know darkness means nanoparticles and not something else?

We don't, from the image alone — which is why the controls matter. A no-magnet control goes through the identical gel, injection, lighting and processing, and stays within ±4.4 L* of zero asymmetry for six hours. The only systematic difference in the magnet arms is the magnet. Also, the darkening is directional: it appears on the magnet side and grows towards it. Cloudiness, drying and lighting drift are not directional, and the asymmetry metric subtracts anything that is symmetric.

The camera exposure changed between photos. Doesn't that ruin everything?

It would, and it is the reason for the floor subtraction. Absolute lightness of identical gel varies by about ±30 L* across this dataset from auto-exposure alone. Every reported value is darkness relative to clean gel in the same photograph, taken as that frame's 15th-percentile interior value. Nothing is ever compared across frames in absolute terms.

Why is the unit of analysis the sample and not the photograph?

Because thirteen photographs of one sample are thirteen looks at the same thing, not thirteen samples. Treating them as independent would give roughly 156 “data points” instead of 12 and would shrink every p-value dramatically and falsely. Each series is collapsed to one plateau value first, and the statistics run on those twelve numbers.

Why an ANOVA rather than four t-tests?

Because the design is a 2 × 2 factorial and the twelve samples all inform the same estimate of noise. Separate pairwise tests each re-estimate noise from two or three samples, leaving two to four degrees of freedom, and throw away most of the available power. The factorial model has eight. This is why the concentration effect appears at p = 0.045 in the correct analysis and appeared non-significant in the earlier pairwise version of the same data.

Did you choose the metric after seeing which one gave a good result?

For the centre-injection analysis, no: asymmetry was defined before any statistics were run, and it is the only sensible summary for a symmetric block with a magnet on one side. For the back-injection analysis the metric was changed — from a mass-weighted centroid to a front position — and the reason is stated openly: back injection leaves a large reservoir pile at the gap, so a mass-weighted statistic scores a magnet arm that has drawn particles into a long tail lower than a control that kept everything heaped. The change was made on that reasoning, was validated against a previously published value for that run, and did not rescue the result — the back comparison is still a null.

Is 0.4% vs 0.6% really a gel effect, or a day effect?

It cannot be separated. 0.4% is Aug 23 and 0.6% is Aug 26. The statistics establish that the two groups differ; they do not establish the cause. Say so explicitly — it is a far stronger position than being asked about it. The one comparison that is within a single day is COOH vs PEG at 0.6% (Aug 26), and that one comes out at p = 0.064.

The coating gap looks much bigger at 0.6%. Why can't you claim that?

Because that claim is a statement about a difference between two differences, which is the interaction term, and it tests at p = 0.38. With three samples per cell there is not enough information to distinguish a genuine interaction from the scatter. Describe the pattern — “COOH led PEG in both concentrations, most markedly at 0.6%” — rather than asserting the concentrations differ in how much coating matters.

How do you know the software found the block correctly every time?

Three ways. Validation sheets render the warped block photograph directly above its heatmap for inspection — that is how the two real geometry failures were found and fixed. The whole analysis was re-run on JPEG-perturbed copies of every photo, changing the result by a median of 0.0015 mm. And on Aug 23 the block was located by two independent methods, gel colour and holder corner marks, whose per-sample rates correlate at r = 0.971 with an identical condition ordering.

Why is the injection gap excluded from the measurement?

The gap holds the undelivered reservoir. Including it would mean the biggest number in the image is the material that has not gone anywhere, and small changes in exactly where the gap edge is detected would swamp the transport signal. A band of ±1.0 mm around the gap is assigned to neither side.

Can you say how much material moved?

No. There is no calibration relating L* to nanoparticle concentration, and the relationship saturates at high loading. Every claim is comparative — more or less, further or shorter — never a quantity. The same applies to depth: it is a top view integrating through the block thickness, so it cannot separate particles distributed through the depth from particles concentrated at one face.

Why does the BSA data look so different in the heatmaps?

BSA gel is intrinsically cloudier — raw darkness around 127 against 67 for non-BSA — so on the shared colour scale BSA controls look almost as bright as BSA magnet arms. That is turbidity, not transport. It is exactly why the asymmetry metric exists: cloudiness is symmetric and cancels. For BSA conditions, cite the asymmetry numbers and treat the heatmap as illustrative.

Was any data excluded?

No sample or timepoint was dropped for its value. The exclusions are all spatial and fixed in advance: a 0.8 mm margin at the block edges where the gel separates from the holder, and ±1.0 mm around the injection gap. The Aug 26 “After injecting” folder is not analysed because t = 0 is defined as the magnet going on. One Aug 27 frame failed geometry detection and was repaired from its condition's median rather than discarded.
