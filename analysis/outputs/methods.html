STAGE 1

The raw data

In: phone photographs  ·  Out: a table of 1005 rows

Every measurement comes from top-view photographs taken with a phone. No instrument other than the camera was used, so most of the pipeline exists to make a phone camera behave like a repeatable one.

A series is one physical sample followed through time, for example COOH_non-BSA_r2. Each series contributes exactly one value to any statistical test. Photographs of the same sample at different times are repeated looks at one thing, not independent samples; treating them as independent would inflate every significance figure.

STAGE 2

Assigning timepoints

In: folder 2.5 hrs/, file PXL_20260823_143012.jpg  ·  Out: nominal hours since the magnet went on

Photographs were filed into one folder per timepoint at capture, and the folder name is the timepoint used throughout (0 hrs, 0.5 hrs, 1 hr …). The capture time embedded in each filename is parsed as well, and used to order shots within a folder, to detect duplicates and re-takes, and to confirm each run's total duration (Aug 26, for instance, spans 6.00 h exactly).

Timepoints are therefore nominal, accurate to the few minutes it took to photograph one full round of samples. For 30-minute sampling over 6 hours this is immaterial.

STAGE 3

Reading which sample each photograph shows

In: photograph  ·  Out: coating, BSA state, repeat number, magnet present

The condition of each sample exists only as handwriting in the frame: coating and BSA state on the sticker, repeat number as tally marks (or L1–L3 / S1–S3 on Aug 23), controls marked “C” or “CTRL”. These were read from the images by a vision model and then verified.

Two features of the rig made reading unreliable and were handled explicitly:

  • Red marker on an orange sticker has very low contrast. Each sticker crop was first transformed to a* − 0.8 b* in LAB colour space, percentile-stretched, inverted and contrast-equalised, which makes marginal text legible.
  • The ribbed magnet strap splits one tally stroke into two or three thin lines, so strokes were counted as groups rather than lines.

How misreads were caught

The design supplies a check the reader does not know about: at every timepoint the three repeats of a condition must be exactly {1, 2, 3}. A trio read as {1, 2, 2} is provably wrong. Every failing trio was re-read against reference crops of each repeat's own tally mark. On Aug 26, 68 photographs went through this referee pass and 25 readings were changed, almost all “II read as III”. All trios on all days now resolve.

Every classification is keyed to the photograph's file path, not its position in a list. Adding one photograph shifts every later index and would silently reassign conditions to the wrong images; keying by path makes that impossible.

STAGE 4

Locating the agarose block

In: photograph, downscaled to 1100 px on the long edge  ·  Out: four corner coordinates

To compare the same spot across photographs, the block must be found in each. Each day uses a different method, because each rig offers different reliable features:

DayBlock is found fromWhy
Aug 23Corner marks drawn on the holder, then series-median repair: a detected quadrilateral that disagrees with its own series' median size or shape is replaced by the median shape The block cannot change size between shots, so a quad that does is a detection failure. Colour segmentation was unstable on this rig; 36 of 196 frames were repaired
Aug 26The four red corner dots on the holder The magnet's warm reflections and the orange sticker both overlap the gel's colour, so colour alone fails
Aug 27Geometry detected once per series from its first frame, then each later frame registered onto it Back injection gives a rigid white rim to anchor on; registering absorbs per-frame detection noise instead of propagating it

The four corners are mapped onto a square by a perspective transform, not a rotation or a bounding rectangle. A square block photographed from a tilted phone is a trapezoid; correcting it with a rotated rectangle shears the image and makes the injection gap lean.

STAGE 5

Pixels to a nanoparticle signal

In: warped 480 × 480 image  ·  Out: a grid of excess-darkness values

Magnetite nanoparticles are dark brown; agarose is pale. Where particles accumulate the gel darkens, and that darkening is the measurement:

  1. Convert to LAB and keep L*, the lightness channel, so the measure responds to how dark a region is rather than to its hue. Darkness is 100 − L* on the CIELAB scale (OpenCV stores L* as 0–255; it is rescaled to 0–100 before any value is reported).
  2. Median-bin in 11 × 11 px cells. The median, not the mean, so a speck of dust, a bubble or a specular highlight cannot pull a cell.
  3. Subtract the frame's own gel floor: the 15th percentile of the block interior.
  4. Light Gaussian smoothing (σ = 1 cell) so the heatmaps read as fields.

Why the floor subtraction is the load-bearing step

A phone sets its own exposure for every shot. Across this dataset the measured lightness of clean gel varies by about 12 L* between frames from auto-exposure alone — comparable to the effects being reported. Any raw-darkness measure would be dominated by it.

Subtracting each frame's own 15th-percentile interior value removes it. Every reported value is therefore darkness above the clean gel in the same photograph, never darkness compared with another photograph.

The 15th percentile is used rather than the minimum because a minimum is a single pixel and therefore noise. The 15th percentile is a robust estimate of the clean-gel background in a frame where part of the block carries nanoparticles.

STAGE 6

The heatmaps

In: excess-darkness grid per photograph  ·  Out: the panels in the report

Each panel is the mean of the three repeats of a condition at one timepoint. The x-axis is millimetres across the block with x = 0 at the block's left edge — where a back-injection gap sits — so centre-injection days put the gap near 5 mm and all three days share one spatial convention.

All three days share one colour scale, 0 to 22 L*. Auto-scaling each panel to its own range makes panels look better and misrepresents relative magnitude; an earlier version did this and made Aug 26 appear dimmer than Aug 23 although Aug 26 has the strongest signal of the three days.

One heatmap artefact to declare

BSA gel is intrinsically cloudier than non-BSA gel — median raw block darkness about 51 against 42. On the shared scale, BSA controls therefore look nearly as bright as BSA magnet arms. That is turbidity, not transport. For BSA conditions the heatmap is illustrative and the asymmetry metric below is the quantity to trust, because cloudiness affects both halves of the block equally and cancels in a difference.

STAGE 7

The asymmetry metric

In: excess-darkness grid  ·  Out: one number per photograph, in L*

This is the number the line graph plots and the statistics test.

gap position
the brightest column band in the middle 40% of the block (the reservoir is the brightest feature there)
exclusion
±1.0 mm around the gap belongs to neither side, so the reservoir cannot drive the result
asymmetry
mean excess darkness on the magnet side minus mean excess darkness on the far side

A difference rather than an amount, because it cancels everything symmetric: gel cloudiness, residual exposure error, BSA turbidity, uneven lighting across the block. What cannot cancel is material that has moved to one side. The magnet is on one side, so directional movement is the signal and everything else is rejected.

This gives the metric a built-in null test. A no-magnet control has no reason to be asymmetric, so its curve should sit at zero — and that check runs on every plot rather than once at calibration.

What the controls did

All four non-BSA controls across both centre-injection days stayed within ±1.7 L* of zero for the full six hours, with no trend. The magnet arms reach 5 to 16 L*. The separation is roughly fivefold, which is why the existence of transport is not in question even where comparisons between conditions are.

STAGE 8

One number per sample

In: 13 timepoints per series  ·  Out: one plateau value per series

Every asymmetry curve rises and flattens by about three hours. The summary value for a series is the mean of its 5 h and 6 h points: its plateau height. Averaging two timepoints halves the influence of one noisy final photograph. A plateau was preferred to a rate because the curves are visibly flat by then; an early-slope measure (0–3 h) gives the same ordering with larger scatter.

This collapses 13 photographs into one number precisely so that the sample, not the photograph, is the unit of replication.

Two factors crossed with each other make this a 2 × 2 factorial, and it is tested as one model. A two-way ANOVA asks three questions of the twelve numbers at once: does concentration matter, does coating matter, and does the effect of one depend on the other.

Why a factorial rather than pairwise t-tests

Separate pairwise tests estimate the noise from only the samples in each pair, leaving two to four degrees of freedom; the factorial model estimates it from all twelve samples and has eight. Same data, more of it used to measure the noise. Analysed pairwise, none of these contrasts reaches significance; analysed as the factorial the design actually is, the concentration effect does.

What p = 0.045 does and does not mean

It means: if agarose concentration made no difference at all, data this lopsided would arise about 4.5% of the time by chance. That is weak-to-moderate evidence, just inside the conventional threshold, and should not be described as strong.

It says nothing about why. In this comparison 0.4% is Aug 23 and 0.6% is Aug 26, so “concentration” and “run day” are the same variable. The statistics establish that the two groups differ; they cannot establish that the gel is the reason. Only running both concentrations on one day could.

The coating comparison within 0.6%

Within Aug 26 alone — one day, no cross-day confound — COOH leads PEG by +4.7 L*, F = 4.44, p = 0.064, 95% confidence interval [−0.3, +9.8], with BSA state as a blocking factor. The BSA block is inert (p = 0.69), which justifies pooling the BSA and non-BSA arms into one test of coating. The interval crosses zero: a consistent direction whose size could be anywhere from nothing to large.

CHECKS

Evidence the pipeline measures the sample, not itself

Four checks, none of which depends on the result coming out a particular way.

1 · Re-encode reproducibility

Every photograph was re-saved as a JPEG — a change of about 1.2 grey levels in 255, invisible to the eye — and the analysis re-run. Median change in the derived depth measure: 0.0015 mm, against effects of 0.1 to 0.16 mm per hour. The measurement is about a hundred times more stable than the effects reported.

2 · Two independent geometries agree

The Aug 23 block was located two unrelated ways, by gel colour and by the holder's corner marks. Per-sample migration rates from the two correlate at r = 0.971 with an identical ordering of conditions. The result does not depend on how the block was found.

3 · Controls sit at zero

±1.7 L* with no trend against signals of 5 to 16 L*. Controls pass through exactly the same code as magnet arms; nothing forces this.

4 · An independent metric reproduces an earlier value

The back-injection depth measure, written separately, recovers the Aug 27 magnet effect at +0.41 mm against the earlier +0.44 mm.

LIMITS

Limitations

  • Agarose concentration is confounded with run day. 0.4% is Aug 23 and 0.6% is Aug 26: different gel batch, different session lighting. The most serious limitation; no analysis removes it.
  • n = 3 per cell. One 0.6% COOH repeat plateaus at 5.0 L* and another at 18.9. That spread is why most contrasts miss significance.
  • The coating × concentration interaction is not supported (p = 0.38). The coating gap looks wider at 0.6% (+7.0 vs +2.5 L*), but that comparison is the one that fails.
  • One cell reverses the coating ordering: Aug 23 small magnet, COOH 7.8 vs PEG 11.8. It is the cell with the least transport of any, but it exists.
  • The back-injection run cannot test any of this. Its own magnet effect is +0.41 mm at p = 0.26; there is nothing for gel stiffness to modulate.
  • Darkness is a proxy, not a concentration. No calibration relates L* to nanoparticle loading, and the relationship saturates towards black. All claims are comparative.
  • A top view integrates through the block. It cannot distinguish particles spread through the thickness from particles concentrated at a face.
  • Timepoints are nominal, to within the minutes taken to photograph a round.

Likely questions

The challenges most likely to be raised, with the answers the data supports.

How do you know darkness means nanoparticles and not something else?

From the controls. A no-magnet control passes through identical gel, injection, lighting and processing, and stays within ±1.7 L* of zero asymmetry for six hours. The only systematic difference in the magnet arms is the magnet. The darkening is also directional: it appears on the magnet side and grows towards it. Cloudiness, drying and lighting drift are not directional, and the asymmetry metric subtracts anything symmetric.

The camera exposure changed between photos. Doesn't that ruin everything?

It would, which is why the floor subtraction exists. Clean-gel lightness varies by about 30 L* across frames from auto-exposure alone. Every reported value is darkness relative to clean gel in the same photograph, taken as that frame's 15th-percentile interior value. Nothing is compared across frames in absolute terms.

Why is the unit of analysis the sample and not the photograph?

Thirteen photographs of one sample are thirteen looks at the same thing. Treating them as independent would give about 156 “data points” instead of 12 and shrink every p-value falsely. Each series is collapsed to one plateau value first, and the statistics run on those twelve numbers.

Why an ANOVA rather than four t-tests?

Because the design is a 2 × 2 factorial and all twelve samples inform the same estimate of noise. Pairwise tests re-estimate noise from two or three samples each, with two to four degrees of freedom, and discard most of the available power; the factorial model has eight. The concentration effect is at p = 0.045 in the correct analysis and non-significant in a pairwise one on the same numbers.

Was the metric chosen after seeing which one gave a result?

For centre injection, no: asymmetry was defined before any statistics were run, and is the natural summary for a symmetric block with a magnet on one side. For back injection the metric was changed, from a mass-weighted centroid to a front position, for a stated physical reason: back injection leaves a large reservoir pile at the gap, so a mass-weighted statistic scores a magnet arm that has drawn particles into a long tail lower than a control that kept everything heaped. The replacement was validated against an earlier published value for that run, and did not change the conclusion — the back comparison is a null under both metrics.

Is 0.4% vs 0.6% a gel effect or a day effect?

The two cannot be separated in this data. The statistics establish that the groups differ; they do not establish the cause. State it explicitly. The one comparison that is within a single day is COOH vs PEG at 0.6% (Aug 26), at p = 0.064.

The coating gap looks much bigger at 0.6%. Why not claim that?

That claim is about a difference between two differences — the interaction term — and it tests at p = 0.38. With three samples per cell a genuine interaction cannot be distinguished from scatter. Describe the pattern (“COOH led PEG in both concentrations, most markedly at 0.6%”) rather than asserting the concentrations differ in how much coating matters.

How do you know the block was found correctly every time?

Three ways. Validation sheets show the warped block photograph directly above its heatmap for inspection, which is how the geometry failures that did occur were found and fixed. The whole analysis was re-run on JPEG-perturbed copies of every photograph and moved by a median of 0.0015 mm. And on Aug 23 two independent detection methods agree at r = 0.971 with an identical ordering of conditions.

Why is the injection gap excluded?

The gap holds the undelivered reservoir. Including it would make the largest value in the image the material that has not gone anywhere, and small changes in the detected gap edge would swamp the transport signal. A band of ±1.0 mm around the gap is assigned to neither side.

Can you say how much material moved?

No. There is no calibration from L* to nanoparticle concentration, and the relationship saturates at high loading. Every claim is comparative. Likewise the top view integrates through the block, so depth measures cannot separate particles distributed through the thickness from particles at one face.

Why does BSA look so different in the heatmaps?

BSA gel is intrinsically cloudier — median raw block darkness about 51 against 42 — so on the shared scale BSA controls look nearly as bright as BSA magnet arms. That is turbidity, not transport, and is exactly what the asymmetry metric cancels. For BSA conditions cite the asymmetry numbers and treat the heatmap as illustrative.

Was any data excluded?

No sample or timepoint was dropped for its value. Spatial exclusions are fixed in advance: a 0.8 mm margin at the block edges where the gel separates from the holder, and ±1.0 mm around the injection gap. The Aug 26 “After injecting” folder is not analysed because t = 0 is defined as the magnet going on. On Aug 27, four photographs of 600 failed the perspective-warp quality check and are omitted from the heatmaps and the back-injection metric (three controls and one magnet frame, at scattered timepoints); none of the twelve values behind the centre-injection ANOVA is affected.
